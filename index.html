How the Calvey and Claude book is made

It is compiled from published research and written to one set of standing rules. The rules exist because the alternative — confident prose with no visible evidence behind it — is how this subject usually gets written.

  • No source text is reproduced. There are no quotations anywhere, in any language. Everything is paraphrased and cited.
  • Every claim carries two grades. First what kind of statement it is — a requirement, a guideline or a research finding. Then where the number came from — measured, simulated, analytical, or a rule of thumb.
  • Every source is marked as opened and confirmed, or as cited from a record without being opened. Nothing rests on the second kind.
  • Conflicts are left standing. Where two studies disagree, both positions are given with their holders named, and no average is taken.
  • Gaps are findings. A figure the research could not reach is marked unfilled and left unfilled, never supplied from memory.
  • Indigenous and community knowledge is cited twice — to the community whose knowledge it is, and to the scholars or practitioners who documented it.
  • No image is used before its licence is read at its source page; each photograph carries its creator, licence and source beside it.

It is written with Claude, and every chapter says so, along with what the research could and could not reach, so a reader can judge the method as well as the findings. The Calvey and ChatGPT book was made separately, to its own method, and is published as it was made.
